Germany Overview

This is an overview of the coffee prices in Germany based on the cappuccino prices extracted from the coffee shops' menus. The data is aggregated to provide insights if the coffee prices correlate with income and tourism.

Issues Encountered

In the funnel diagram you can see that out of 12,982 initial coffeeshops, more than half were filtered out since they had no website listed on OSM. Of the 5,429 coffeeshops that did have a website, we extracted prices for only 610. This big drop was caused by multiple issues:

  • Social Media Blacklist: Many coffeeshops link to their social media page rather than their own website. To avoid login walls and conform to ToS, our web-scraper skipped these websites completely.
  • Menu Finder: Many websites simply did not publish their menu online, or our scraper was unable to find it.
  • Price Extraction: Even when a menu was found, these were inconsitently structured and extracting the prices automatically is challenging as adjusting the script for one menu could break it for others. There was also no reliable way of telling whether the price extracted was actually correct. For validation we made use of Data Cleaning.
  • OSM Crowdsourcing: Since OpenStreetMap relies on user contributions, not every coffee shop is listed there. Another issue is the incorrect labeling of businesses that are not coffee shops but are tagged as such.

Results

The map above visualizes the average cappuccino prices in different cities across Germany. Cities with unreliable price data are shown in a dimmed color.

The two maps above show the explanatory variables themselves: disposable income per person and overnight stays per inhabitant. Both come from the official city statistics, so every city with a published value is included.

The scatter plots above show that there is no clear correlation between cappuccino prices and disposable income or tourism.

Both maps show how far each city's cappuccino price lies above (red) or below (blue) the price predicted by a linear fit on disposable income and on overnight stays. Only cities with a reliable sample size are included.

This map divides the yearly disposable income per person by the local cappuccino price, so each city shows how many cappuccinos an average inhabitant could buy from one year of disposable income. It combines both effects: a city is dark either because coffee is cheap or because incomes are high. Cities with unreliable price data are shown in a dimmed color.

Conclusion

Going back to our research questions:

RQ1: Can a representative CoffeeIndex be created based on publically available information?

The success in constructing the CoffeeIndex is rather limited. Mainly constrained by data sources to the webpresences of the coffee shops themselves and barred from using commercial and data rich ressources like Google maps we only achieved around 5% percent coverage of coffeeshops.
For big cities like berlin with 88 extracted cappucino prices, this may be a statistically significant subset resilient to outliers and extraction errors. Cities like Bochum, where our data set dwindled down to 3 extracted cappucino prices, allow for little dependable results. A single cafes peculiar pricing decision or extraction error is bound to sway the results beyond reliability.

Sidegoal: "Where can we find the cheapest coffee in a city of our choosing?"

Our (secondary) research question regarding a useful overview of coffee pricing within a singe city is similarily impaired by the legal restraints described above. While the resulting map may point one to a neat new coffee shop for an extended coworking session with many a cheap coffee, it is likely to not be the cheapest.

RQ2: How reliable is the CoffeeIndex as a proxy for economic metrics?

In answering our last research question we are aided by the fact, that we can reduce our analysis to those cities that had enough coffee shops with an extractable price and look for a correlation among these. Even for these cities we can not see a clear correlation between cappucino prices and metrics like median income or the tourism metric.
